# Distinctive Signals

Some experiences are not directly related to the target role, but they may still be worth keeping because they make the candidate easier to remember or show how the candidate builds trust in real groups.

## Keep Only When They Prove Something

Ask what the experience proves:

- Social trust.
- Helping behavior.
- Team integration.
- Craft and precision.
- Long-term curiosity.
- Cross-domain transfer.
- Communication and relationship-building.

If the answer is only "it is interesting", keep it out of the resume or move it to a very low-priority section.

## Example: Basic Acupuncture Practice

Weak:

```text
Skilled in acupuncture and able to improve others' physical discomfort.
```

Problems:

- Sounds like a medical claim.
- May invite uncomfortable or high-risk follow-up questions.
- Does not connect to team behavior.

Safer:

```text
Basic acupuncture theory and practice; used in campus team settings to provide basic relaxation support and build trust in collaborative environments.
```

Chinese version:

```text
中医针灸基础理论与实操学习经历；曾在校内团队活动中为同学、老师提供基础放松支持，帮助建立信任与融入协作环境。
```

## Placement

Use low-priority sections:

- Additional credentials.
- Personal traits.
- Community participation.
- Other experience.

Do not place it above core role evidence unless the target role directly benefits from it, such as medical device manufacturing, rehabilitation robotics, healthcare operations, or human factors.
