# Distinctive Signals and Low-Material Mining

Use this reference when the user has few obvious resume materials, says they have "nothing to write", or mentions an unusual experience that seems unrelated to the target role.

## Low-Material Mining

Do not assume a student has no value because they lack internships, awards, or large projects. Search for evidence in:

- Course labs, reports, course design, final assignments, and practical training.
- Club, class, volunteer, competition, or event micro-tasks.
- Documentation, equipment checks, meeting notes, data organization, onboarding help, or on-site support.
- Small repairs, builds, simulations, debugging attempts, demos, or personal tools.
- AI-assisted learning cases where the user can explain the prompt/input, verification, output, and how it changed their work.
- Helping others solve concrete problems.
- Unusual personal skills that build trust, memorability, precision, service mindset, or team integration.

For each candidate item, ask:

```text
What did you do?
What tool, method, or skill did you use?
Who benefited?
What changed or what was delivered?
What enterprise value does this imply?
Can you explain it in an interview?
```

## Activity Is Not Evidence

Do not write "participated in many activities" as a value claim. Convert only the user's real contribution:

| Weak | Better |
| --- | --- |
| Participated in many campus activities and improved comprehensive ability | In campus events, handled check-in, material distribution, and temporary coordination tasks, supporting smooth on-site execution |
| Joined a club and learned teamwork | Organized meeting notes and resource lists for a student team, reducing repeated communication and helping new members catch up |

## Enterprise Value Translation

When a user has few hard skills, look for value that a real organization can understand:

- Execution value: can complete basic tasks reliably.
- Communication cost value: responds clearly, documents work, and reduces repeated explanation.
- Team trust value: helps others and becomes easy to work with.
- Organizing value: coordinates people, materials, schedules, or information.
- Fast adaptation value: enters unfamiliar tasks and produces a usable output.
- Scarcity/recognition value: has a memorable skill or background that makes the candidate easier to remember.

## Unusual Experiences

Do not mechanically delete experiences just because they are not directly related to the target role. Keep them only when they prove at least one of:

- Personal recognition.
- Scarcity or memorability.
- Helping behavior.
- Team integration.
- Trust-building in a new group.
- Service mindset.
- Craft, precision, or body/hand coordination.
- Cross-domain learning.

If the answer is only "it is interesting", omit it or move it to a low-priority section.

## Example: Basic Acupuncture Practice

Weak:

```text
Skilled in acupuncture and able to improve others' physical discomfort.
```

Problems:

- Sounds like a medical claim.
- May invite high-risk medical follow-up questions.
- Does not connect to team value.

Safer:

```text
Basic acupuncture theory and practice; used in campus team settings to provide basic relaxation support and build trust in collaborative environments.
```

Chinese:

```text
中医针灸基础理论与实操学习经历；曾在校内团队活动中为同学、老师提供基础放松支持，帮助建立信任与融入协作环境。
```

Use low-priority sections such as additional credentials, personal traits, community participation, or other experience. Do not place it above core role evidence unless the target role directly benefits from it, such as medical device manufacturing, rehabilitation robotics, healthcare operations, or human factors.

## Safety Boundary

For health-related experiences, do not claim:

- Treatment, cure, diagnosis, or medical intervention.
- Guaranteed physical improvement.
- Professional medical qualification unless the user has a real credential and wants to disclose it.

Use only conservative wording around learning experience, basic support, helping behavior, and trust-building.
